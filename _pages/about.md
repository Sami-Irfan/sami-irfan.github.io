# About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Civil, Construction, and Environmental Engineering at **The University of Alabama**, working under the supervision of **Dr. Mizanur Rahman**. My research focuses on the security and resilience of positioning and navigation systems, particularly for connected and autonomous vehicles.

My doctoral research investigates vulnerabilities in **Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS)**, with an emphasis on spoofing attacks, and develops approaches for detecting navigation anomalies and maintaining reliable vehicle localization under adversarial conditions. My work integrates GNSS, inertial navigation systems, LiDAR, and digital map information to enhance navigation integrity and resilience.

Beyond GNSS security and autonomous navigation, my broader research interests include intelligent transportation systems, connected and automated mobility, transportation cybersecurity, and digital twin technologies. My overarching goal is to contribute to safer and more reliable autonomous transportation systems.

## Research Interests

### GNSS Security and Resilient Navigation

I investigate vulnerabilities affecting satellite-based positioning and navigation, particularly GNSS spoofing and interference. My research develops approaches to detect navigation anomalies and maintain reliable vehicle navigation in environments where GNSS measurements may be compromised.

### Multisensor Fusion and Autonomous Vehicle Localization

I develop sensor fusion and localization methods that combine GNSS, inertial navigation, LiDAR, and digital map information. My interests include probabilistic estimation and map-aided localization for accurate, robust, and continuous autonomous vehicle navigation.
